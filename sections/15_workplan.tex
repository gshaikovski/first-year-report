\chapter{Workplan}
\label{ch:workplan}

The workplan is organised into two- to three-month blocks and follows the order of the research questions in Chapter~\ref{ch:proposal}, since each question depends on the results of the previous one.
The second year (October 2026 to September 2027) is dedicated to the learning-regimes project in Section~\ref{sec:proposal_rq1}, which addresses Research Question~1, and to the baselines and the interpretability study of the trace-mechanism project in Section~\ref{sec:proposal_rq2}.
The third year (October 2027 to September 2028) completes the trace-mechanism project, which addresses Research Question~2, and carries out the length-generalisation project in Section~\ref{sec:proposal_rq3}, which addresses Research Question~3.
Thesis writing begins in August 2028, in parallel with the final experiments, and continues into the fourth year until submission in January 2029.

The main milestones are the submission of three papers, one per research question and core chapter of the thesis, to leading machine learning conferences (NeurIPS, ICML, and ICLR).
Each paper is prepared in three stages: (1) a workshop submission presenting preliminary results and obtaining initial peer-review feedback; (2) submission to the main conference track; and (3) an optional resubmission to the subsequent conference in the event of rejection.
The first paper, \emph{Is In-Context Search Worth Its Compute?}, is prepared for submission to ICLR 2027 and is therefore allocated only the optional resubmission stage.
Table~\ref{tab:workplan} presents the detailed workplan, including these milestones alongside the anticipated work for each period.

\wpyear{Year 2 (October 2026 -- September 2027)}

Nov--Dec 2026 &
\wprqone{Learning-regimes project (RQ1): task suite.}
Add search problems to the task suite beyond maze navigation and Countdown, covering different combinations of the candidate factors in the problem structure (in-context graph, locality of the transition function, presence of useful heuristics).
Run the matched-compute evaluations on each new task to confirm that the regime definition holds and that the same scaling curves appear.
Formalise the regimes missing from the current definition and check whether any of them appear in practice. \\

Jan--Feb 2027 &
\wprqone{Learning-regimes project (RQ1): theory.}
Translate the predictions of the learnability theory reviewed in Section~\ref{sec:bg_reasoning} (globality, RASP-L expressibility, locality of dependencies) into measurable task properties in the suite.
Reproduce, or fail to reproduce, the predicted regimes on the suite, and derive educated hypotheses about the factors that determine the regime. \\

\wpmilestone Jan 2027 &
\textbf{ICML 2027 submission (optional).}
If rejected from ICLR 2027, revise \emph{Is In-Context Search Worth Its Compute?} in response to reviews and resubmit. \\

\wprqone{Learning-regimes project (RQ1): causal factors.}
Test the hypothesised factors by controlled ablations and interventions on the task structure.
Iterate between testing a hypothesis, reviewing the theory, and deriving a new hypothesis when a factor has no effect, and extend the theory where its predictions fail on search problems. \\

Jul--Aug 2027 &
\wprqtwo{Trace-mechanism project (RQ2): baselines.}
Implement alternative forms of intermediate computation (content-free tokens, unrelated and corrupted traces, continuous latent thoughts, looped and recurrent-depth models) and evaluate them against SA-CoT and SO models at matched compute on one or two tasks in the search-efficient regime, selected from the task suite of RQ1.
Apply existing interpretability methods to SA-CoT models and to the most promising alternatives to begin identifying the mechanisms behind their efficiency. \\

Sep--Oct 2027 &
\wprqtwo{Trace-mechanism project (RQ2): mechanisms.}
Iterate on the interpretability methods for search traces to isolate the causal mechanisms behind their efficiency in the search-efficient regime; analyse how search is represented and executed in the model: which components of the trace the model attends to, what its internal representations encode about the search state, and whether it executes the transition and expansion rules of the algorithm. \\
